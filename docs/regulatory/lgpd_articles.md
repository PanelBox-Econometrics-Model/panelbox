# LGPD Key Articles: Detailed Analysis

## Introduction

LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados Pessoais) contains 65 articles spanning the full lifecycle of personal data processing in Brazil. This document provides a detailed analysis of the most significant articles, covering definitions, principles, legal bases, consent, legitimate interest, children's data, data subject rights, automated decision-making, and international transfers. LGPD is supervised by ANPD (Autoridade Nacional de Protecao de Dados), and compliance with these articles is mandatory for all controllers and processors operating within the scope of the law.

## LGPD_Article_5: Definitions

LGPD_Article_5 is the definitional article that establishes the terminological foundation for the entire law. This article contains 19 definitions (incisos I through XIX) that are referenced throughout all other articles.

The most critical definitions in LGPD_Article_5 include:

- **Inciso I - Personal data (dado pessoal)**: Information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. This broad definition means that any data that can be linked back to an individual, directly or indirectly, falls within LGPD scope.
- **Inciso II - Sensitive personal data (dado pessoal sensivel)**: Data concerning racial or ethnic origin, religious conviction, political opinion, trade union or religious, philosophical, or political organization membership, data concerning health or sexual life, genetic or biometric data when linked to a natural person.
- **Inciso V - Consent (consentimento)**: Free, informed, and unambiguous manifestation by which the data subject agrees to the processing of their personal data for a defined purpose.
- **Inciso VI - Controller (controlador)**: Natural or legal person, public or private, to whom decisions regarding the processing of personal data are attributed.
- **Inciso VII - Processor (operador)**: Natural or legal person, public or private, that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.
- **Inciso VIII - DPO (encarregado)**: Person appointed by the controller and processor to act as a communication channel between the controller, data subjects, and ANPD.
- **Inciso X - Processing (tratamento)**: Any operation performed on personal data, covering 20 enumerated activities from collection to extraction.
- **Inciso XII - Shared use of data (uso compartilhado)**: Communication, diffusion, international transfer, or interconnection of personal data with other controllers or processors.
- **Inciso XIV - Elimination (eliminacao)**: Deletion of data or set of data stored in a database.

LGPD_Article_5 establishes that the scope of LGPD is deliberately broad, covering virtually any operation involving data that can identify a natural person.

## LGPD_Article_6: Principles

LGPD_Article_6 establishes ten binding principles that govern all personal data processing under LGPD. These principles are not merely aspirational; they are enforceable requirements that ANPD assesses during supervisory actions and that form the basis for administrative sanctions under LGPD_Article_52.

The ten principles of LGPD_Article_6 are: purpose (finalidade), adequacy (adequacao), necessity (necessidade), free access (livre acesso), data quality (qualidade dos dados), transparency (transparencia), security (seguranca), prevention (prevencao), non-discrimination (nao discriminacao), and accountability (responsabilizacao e prestacao de contas).

The necessity principle is particularly relevant for financial institutions subject to Basel_III requirements: while KYC and AML processes require extensive data collection, the necessity principle demands that only data strictly necessary for these compliance purposes be collected and retained.

The accountability principle requires controllers to demonstrate compliance with all other principles. This creates a documentation obligation that intersects with the DPIA requirement under LGPD_Article_38 and the security measures obligation under LGPD_Article_46.

## LGPD_Article_7: Legal Bases for Processing

LGPD_Article_7 defines the ten legal bases that authorize the processing of personal data. Processing without a valid legal basis constitutes a violation of LGPD enforceable by ANPD through sanctions under LGPD_Article_52.

The ten legal bases under LGPD_Article_7 are:

1. **Consent** (LGPD_Article_7, I): The data subject's consent, which must meet the requirements of LGPD_Article_8.
2. **Legal obligation** (LGPD_Article_7, II): Compliance with a legal or regulatory obligation of the controller. This basis is critical for financial institutions complying with Basel_III, AML regulations, and BCB requirements.
3. **Public policy** (LGPD_Article_7, III): Execution of public policies by the public administration.
4. **Research** (LGPD_Article_7, IV): Conducting studies by research bodies, with anonymization whenever possible.
5. **Contract** (LGPD_Article_7, V): Performance of a contract or preliminary procedures related to a contract to which the data subject is a party.
6. **Exercise of rights** (LGPD_Article_7, VI): Exercise of rights in judicial, administrative, or arbitration proceedings.
7. **Protection of life** (LGPD_Article_7, VII): Protection of life or physical safety.
8. **Health protection** (LGPD_Article_7, VIII): Health protection procedures by health professionals or entities.
9. **Legitimate interest** (LGPD_Article_7, IX): Legitimate interest of the controller or third party, subject to the test established by LGPD_Article_10.
10. **Credit protection** (LGPD_Article_7, X): Credit protection purposes. This basis is unique to LGPD (not present in GDPR) and is particularly relevant for the financial sector's use of credit scoring models involving PD and other risk parameters.

## LGPD_Article_8: Consent

LGPD_Article_8 details the requirements for valid consent under LGPD. Consent must be:

- Provided in writing or by another means demonstrating the data subject's will
- If in writing, included in a clause clearly distinguished from other contractual provisions
- Specific to defined purposes; generic authorizations for processing are void
- Freely given, meaning consent obtained through coercion or conditioned on the provision of goods/services is invalid

LGPD_Article_8 further provides that the burden of proof regarding consent lies with the controller. Consent may be revoked at any time through a free and facilitated procedure, and the revocation does not affect processing carried out under previously given consent. If there are changes to the purpose or form of processing, the controller must inform the data subject and obtain new consent.

The consent requirements under LGPD_Article_8 interact with LGPD_Article_7 (consent as a legal basis), LGPD_Article_5 (definition of consent), and LGPD_Article_18 (right to revoke consent).

## LGPD_Article_10: Legitimate Interest

LGPD_Article_10 establishes the conditions for processing based on the legitimate interest of the controller. The legitimate interest basis under LGPD_Article_7, IX is subject to specific safeguards:

- Processing must be for legitimate purposes, considered from concrete situations, which include (but are not limited to): support and promotion of the controller's activities, and protection of the data subject's exercise of rights or provision of services.
- Only data strictly necessary for the intended purpose may be processed (reinforcing the necessity principle of LGPD_Article_6).
- ANPD may request a DPIA (LGPD_Article_38) from controllers that process data on the basis of legitimate interest.

LGPD_Article_10 requires a balancing test between the controller's legitimate interest and the data subject's rights and expectations. This is analogous to the legitimate interest assessment under GDPR Article 6(1)(f).

For financial institutions, LGPD_Article_10 is relevant for activities such as fraud prevention, risk assessment, and internal audit processes that may not fall neatly under other legal bases.

## LGPD_Article_14: Children's and Adolescents' Data

LGPD_Article_14 establishes specific protections for the processing of personal data of children and adolescents. Processing must be carried out in the best interest of the child. For children (under 12), processing requires specific and prominent consent from at least one parent or legal guardian. Controllers must make reasonable efforts to verify that consent was provided by the parent/guardian, considering available technology. Information about data processing must be provided in a simple, clear, and accessible manner appropriate for the child's understanding.

## LGPD_Article_18: Data Subject Rights

LGPD_Article_18 is one of the most impactful provisions of LGPD, establishing a comprehensive catalog of rights that data subjects may exercise against the controller. Data subjects have the right to obtain from the controller, at any time and upon request:

1. **Confirmation** of the existence of processing
2. **Access** to their data
3. **Correction** of incomplete, inaccurate, or outdated data
4. **Anonymization, blocking, or elimination** of unnecessary, excessive, or non-compliant data
5. **Portability** of data to another service provider or product
6. **Deletion** of personal data processed with consent (when consent is revoked)
7. **Information** about public and private entities with which data has been shared
8. **Information** about the possibility of denying consent and the consequences thereof
9. **Revocation of consent** under LGPD_Article_8

These rights must be exercised through an express request by the data subject to the controller. ANPD receives complaints from data subjects whose rights have been denied. Non-compliance with LGPD_Article_18 may result in sanctions under LGPD_Article_52.

## LGPD_Article_20: Automated Decision-Making

LGPD_Article_20 addresses decisions made solely through automated processing of personal data. The data subject has the right to request review of decisions made solely on the basis of automated processing that affect their interests, including decisions intended to define their personal, professional, consumer, or credit profile or aspects of their personality.

The controller must provide clear and adequate information regarding the criteria and procedures used for the automated decision upon request. If the controller cannot provide this information due to commercial or industrial secrecy, ANPD may conduct an audit to verify discriminatory aspects in automated processing.

LGPD_Article_20 is highly relevant for the financial sector, where automated models using PD, LGD, and other Credit_Risk parameters make credit decisions. Banks must be prepared to explain the logic of their automated credit scoring systems to data subjects and ANPD.

## LGPD_Article_33: International Data Transfer

LGPD_Article_33 establishes the conditions under which personal data may be transferred to foreign countries or international organizations. International transfers are permitted only when:

1. The receiving country or organization provides an adequate degree of data protection (as determined by ANPD)
2. The controller offers and proves adequate guarantees through contractual clauses, standard contractual provisions, or binding corporate rules
3. The transfer is necessary for international legal cooperation
4. The transfer is necessary for protection of life or physical safety
5. ANPD has authorized the transfer
6. The transfer is the subject of an international commitment by Brazil
7. The transfer is necessary for the execution of public policy
8. The data subject has given specific and prominent consent
9. The transfer is necessary for compliance with a legal or regulatory obligation, contract performance, or exercise of rights

LGPD_Article_33 mirrors GDPR's international transfer mechanisms (adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules) but is implemented by ANPD rather than by the European Commission or national Data Protection Authorities.

For global financial institutions subject to both LGPD and GDPR, international data transfers require careful analysis to ensure compliance with both frameworks simultaneously. Similarly, Basel_III Pillar_3 disclosures involving personal data must consider LGPD_Article_33 restrictions.

## Conclusion

The articles analyzed in this document form the core of the LGPD framework supervised by ANPD. LGPD_Article_5 provides the definitional foundation, LGPD_Article_6 establishes binding principles, LGPD_Article_7 defines the legal bases for processing, LGPD_Article_8 specifies consent requirements, LGPD_Article_10 governs legitimate interest, LGPD_Article_14 protects children's data, LGPD_Article_18 enumerates data subject rights, LGPD_Article_20 addresses automated decisions, and LGPD_Article_33 regulates international transfers. Together, these provisions create a comprehensive data protection regime that impacts all sectors, particularly financial institutions operating under Basel_III and other regulatory frameworks.
