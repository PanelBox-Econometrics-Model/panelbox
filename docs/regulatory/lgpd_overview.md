# LGPD Overview: Brazil's General Data Protection Law

## Introduction

LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados Pessoais) is Brazil's comprehensive data protection legislation, enacted as Federal Law No. 13,709/2018 and effective since September 2020. LGPD establishes rules for the collection, processing, storage, and sharing of personal data, applying to any natural person or legal entity that processes personal data in Brazil, regardless of where the processor is based. LGPD is supervised and enforced by ANPD (Autoridade Nacional de Protecao de Dados), the Brazilian National Data Protection Authority. LGPD draws significant inspiration from GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), the European Union's data protection framework, while incorporating provisions specific to the Brazilian legal context.

## LGPD Structure

LGPD is organized into 65 articles across 10 chapters:

- **Chapter I (Articles 1-6)**: Preliminary provisions, scope, and principles
- **Chapter II (Articles 7-16)**: Processing of personal data, legal bases, and sensitive data
- **Chapter III (Articles 17-22)**: Rights of data subjects
- **Chapter IV (Articles 23-32)**: Processing by the public sector
- **Chapter V (Articles 33-36)**: International transfer of personal data
- **Chapter VI (Articles 37-40)**: Processing agents (controller and processor)
- **Chapter VII (Articles 41-45)**: Data protection officer and governance
- **Chapter VIII (Articles 46-51)**: Security and good practices
- **Chapter IX (Articles 52-54)**: Administrative sanctions
- **Chapter X (Articles 55-65)**: ANPD establishment and final provisions

## Key Definitions Under LGPD_Article_5

LGPD_Article_5 provides the foundational definitions used throughout the law. Key definitions include:

- **Personal data**: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.
- **Sensitive personal data**: Personal data concerning racial or ethnic origin, religious belief, political opinion, trade union membership, health or sexual life data, genetic or biometric data.
- **Data subject (titular)**: The natural person to whom the personal data refers.
- **Controller (controlador)**: The natural person or legal entity that makes decisions regarding the processing of personal data.
- **Processor (operador)**: The natural person or legal entity that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.
- **Data Protection Officer (encarregado)**: The person appointed by the controller to act as a communication channel between the controller, data subjects, and ANPD.
- **Processing (tratamento)**: Any operation performed on personal data, including collection, production, reception, classification, utilization, access, reproduction, transmission, distribution, processing, archiving, storage, elimination, evaluation, control, modification, communication, transfer, diffusion, or extraction.

These definitions from LGPD_Article_5 establish the terminological foundation for the entire regulatory framework and are essential for interpreting all subsequent articles.

## Principles of LGPD

LGPD_Article_6 establishes ten principles that govern all personal data processing activities:

1. **Purpose (finalidade)**: Processing must be carried out for legitimate, specific, explicit, and informed purposes.
2. **Adequacy (adequacao)**: Processing must be compatible with the stated purposes.
3. **Necessity (necessidade)**: Processing must be limited to the minimum necessary for its stated purpose.
4. **Free access (livre acesso)**: Data subjects must have easy and free access to information about the processing of their data.
5. **Data quality (qualidade dos dados)**: Accuracy, clarity, relevance, and currency of data must be guaranteed.
6. **Transparency (transparencia)**: Clear, precise, and easily accessible information about the processing must be provided.
7. **Security (seguranca)**: Technical and administrative measures must protect personal data from unauthorized access and accidental or unlawful situations.
8. **Prevention (prevencao)**: Measures must be adopted to prevent damages from personal data processing.
9. **Non-discrimination (nao discriminacao)**: Processing must not be carried out for unlawful or abusive discriminatory purposes.
10. **Accountability and compliance (responsabilizacao e prestacao de contas)**: The agent must demonstrate the adoption of effective measures capable of proving compliance.

## Legal Bases for Processing Under LGPD_Article_7

LGPD_Article_7 defines ten legal bases for processing personal data:

1. Consent of the data subject (LGPD_Article_8 details consent requirements)
2. Compliance with a legal or regulatory obligation
3. Execution of public policies by the public administration
4. Conducting studies by research bodies (with anonymization when possible)
5. Performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party
6. Exercise of rights in judicial, administrative, or arbitration proceedings
7. Protection of life or physical safety of the data subject or third party
8. Health protection in procedures carried out by health professionals or health entities
9. Legitimate interest of the controller or third party (LGPD_Article_10 details the legitimate interest test)
10. Credit protection

## Controller vs. Processor

LGPD establishes a clear distinction between the controller and the processor, as defined in LGPD_Article_5. The controller determines the purposes and means of processing and bears primary responsibility for compliance with LGPD. The processor acts on behalf of and under the instructions of the controller. Both are considered "processing agents" (agentes de tratamento).

The controller is responsible for:
- Defining the legal basis for processing under LGPD_Article_7
- Appointing the Data Protection Officer (as required by LGPD_Article_41)
- Conducting DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment) when required under LGPD_Article_38
- Notifying ANPD and data subjects in case of security incidents (LGPD_Article_48)
- Responding to data subject rights requests (LGPD_Article_18)

The processor must:
- Process data only according to the controller's instructions
- Implement appropriate security measures under LGPD_Article_46
- Maintain records of processing activities
- Cooperate with ANPD in supervisory matters

## LGPD and GDPR Comparison

LGPD and GDPR share significant structural similarities, reflecting LGPD's inspiration from the European model. Both frameworks:

- Establish comprehensive rules for personal data processing
- Define similar categories of personal data and sensitive data
- Require a legal basis for processing
- Grant data subjects rights of access, rectification, deletion, and portability
- Mandate data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing
- Impose notification requirements for data breaches
- Establish independent supervisory authorities (ANPD for LGPD, national Data Protection Authorities for GDPR)
- Provide for significant administrative sanctions

Key differences between LGPD and GDPR include:

- **Legal bases**: LGPD provides ten legal bases under LGPD_Article_7, while GDPR provides six. LGPD uniquely includes credit protection as a legal basis.
- **Sensitive data**: LGPD_Article_11 provides separate legal bases for sensitive data processing, whereas GDPR Article 9 uses a derogation model.
- **Data Protection Officer**: LGPD_Article_41 requires all controllers to appoint a DPO, whereas GDPR limits the DPO requirement to specific circumstances.
- **Sanctions**: LGPD_Article_52 provides for administrative sanctions up to 2% of revenue in Brazil (capped at BRL 50 million per infraction), while GDPR allows fines up to 4% of global annual turnover.
- **International transfers**: LGPD_Article_33 regulates international data transfers with mechanisms similar to but not identical to GDPR.
- **Supervisory authority**: ANPD supervises LGPD as a single national authority, while GDPR has multiple national supervisory authorities coordinated by the European Data Protection Board.

## ANPD Role and Powers

ANPD is the supervisory authority responsible for overseeing, implementing, and enforcing LGPD. ANPD was established by LGPD itself and became operational in 2020. ANPD's key responsibilities include:

- Issuing regulations and guidelines for LGPD implementation
- Monitoring compliance and conducting audits
- Receiving and processing complaints from data subjects
- Applying administrative sanctions under LGPD_Article_52
- Promoting public awareness of data protection
- Cooperating with data protection authorities of other countries (including GDPR supervisory authorities)
- Requesting DPIA from controllers when processing presents risks (LGPD_Article_38)
- Establishing rules for international data transfers (LGPD_Article_33)

## Intersection with Financial Regulation

LGPD has significant implications for the financial sector, which is also subject to Basel_III requirements supervised by the BCB. Banks processing customer personal data must comply with both LGPD and banking secrecy regulations. Key intersection points include:

- **KYC (Know Your Customer)** processes require collection of personal data, which must comply with LGPD principles and have an appropriate legal basis under LGPD_Article_7.
- **AML (Anti-Money Laundering)** compliance involves processing personal and potentially sensitive data, creating tension with data minimization principles.
- **Pillar_3 disclosures** under Basel_III must be balanced against LGPD data protection requirements.
- **Automated decision-making**: LGPD_Article_20 grants data subjects the right to review automated decisions, which affects credit scoring models that use PD, LGD, and other risk parameters.
- **DPIA requirements**: Financial institutions processing data at scale must conduct DPIA under LGPD_Article_38, assessed by ANPD.

## Conclusion

LGPD represents Brazil's comprehensive approach to personal data protection, supervised by ANPD and sharing significant structural similarities with GDPR. Through its principles-based framework, ten legal bases for processing, and robust data subject rights, LGPD establishes a regulatory environment that affects all sectors, including financial institutions subject to Basel_III and other prudential frameworks. The definitions in LGPD_Article_5, principles in LGPD_Article_6, and legal bases in LGPD_Article_7 form the foundational pillars of the law.
